# Framework cheatsheet

## Self-Determination Theory (SDT)
- **Source**: Deci & Ryan, "Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior" (1985); SDT meta-analyses
- **Use for**: engagement, retention, gamification critique, why intrinsic motivation beats extrinsic over time
- **Constructs**: Autonomy (choice / control), Competence (mastery / progress), Relatedness (connection / care)
- **Signal**: when one of these is thwarted, engagement degrades even if surface metrics look fine

## Dual-process theory
- **Source**: Kahneman, "Thinking, Fast and Slow" (2011) — popularized; underlying research is older (Stanovich, Evans)
- **Use for**: UX defaults, friction design, cognitive load
- **Constructs**: System 1 (fast, automatic, biased), System 2 (slow, deliberate, lazy)
- **Signal**: when a decision is high-stakes but flows through System 1 (defaults, time pressure), errors increase

## Transtheoretical / Stages of Change
- **Source**: Prochaska & DiClemente (1983) for smoking cessation; broadened since
- **Use for**: behavior-change products (health, fitness, finance)
- **Stages**: precontemplation → contemplation → preparation → action → maintenance
- **Signal**: pushing "action" content to someone in precontemplation = wasted; tailor content to stage

## COM-B
- **Source**: Michie et al. (2011), "The Behaviour Change Wheel"
- **Use for**: designing interventions to change a specific behavior
- **Model**: Capability (psych + phys) + Opportunity (social + physical) + Motivation (reflective + automatic) → Behavior
- **Signal**: if a desired behavior isn't happening, diagnose which of C / O / M is the blocker before designing the nudge

## Self-efficacy
- **Source**: Bandura (1977); extensive replication
- **Use for**: onboarding, learning curves, churn at first failure
- **Constructs**: belief in one's ability to perform a behavior; built via mastery experiences > vicarious > verbal persuasion
- **Signal**: users who fail their first attempt at a core task have ~50% lower retention; design for early wins

## Attachment
- **Source**: Bowlby; Ainsworth; Mikulincer & Shaver for adult attachment in non-clinical contexts
- **Use for**: long-term relationships (community, support, premium customer success)
- **Patterns**: secure / anxious / avoidant — describe engagement patterns, not pathology
- **Signal**: customers who churn quietly without complaining = avoidant pattern; outreach style differs from anxious

## Big Five (OCEAN)
- **Source**: extensive trait psychology consensus (Costa & McCrae)
- **Use for**: persona variation that holds across cultures
- **Dimensions**: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism
- **Caution**: dimensions, not types. Don't say "she's high-openness type."
