# Cultural accuracy — common misrepresentations to watch

## General principle
Cultural representation is specific, not aggregated. "Asian," "African," "Latinx," "Middle Eastern" are not single categories. Specify the country / region / community when it matters.

## Common AI defaults to override

### South Asian
- Model defaults to bright, saturated festival clothing as everyday wear. Most South Asian people wear everyday clothes most of the time. Specify context.
- Hindu, Muslim, Sikh, Christian, Jain communities have distinct visual markers. Don't blend.
- Skin tone variation is enormous; "South Asian" doesn't mean one shade.

### East Asian
- Don't default Japanese, Chinese, Korean visual cues into one. Architecture, dress, food, naming conventions differ.
- "Geisha" / "samurai" / "ninja" framing for modern Japanese subjects is stereotype.
- Bowing, chopsticks, cherry blossoms as ambient set-dressing reads as exoticism.

### African / African-diaspora
- "African" lumps a continent of 54 countries. Specify (Nigerian, Kenyan, Ethiopian, Ghanaian, etc.) when relevant.
- Skin tone variation within any single African nation is huge. "Dark-skinned" is not a complete description.
- Don't default to traditional dress (kente, dashiki) for modern professional settings unless contextually correct.

### Middle Eastern / North African
- Religious dress is everyday; not a costume.
- Hijab, niqab, abaya, kaftan, thobe have specific cultural origins; don't blend.
- Don't default to desert settings; most MENA people live in cities.

### Latin American / Hispanic
- "Latino/a/x" includes Indigenous, Afro-Latino, mestizo, European-descent populations. Skin tone, features vary enormously.
- Don't default to mariachi / sombrero / serape for modern Mexican subjects.

### Indigenous (multiple continents)
- Specify nation/people, not "Native American" / "Aboriginal" / "First Nations" alone.
- Ceremonial dress is not everyday dress and is often sacred. Don't render lightly.

### Religious dress (across traditions)
- Render in everyday context (workplace, café, gym) as ordinary, not exoticized.
- Don't put one religion's dress on a person of another visible heritage as "creative mashup."

## Reality check
- Show the visual to someone from the depicted community before publishing. If your team doesn't include that voice, find an external reviewer for high-stakes campaigns.
