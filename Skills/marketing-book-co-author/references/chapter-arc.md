# Chapter arc — shapes that hold

## Claim → tension → reframe (the standard)
1. Open with a claim or a story.
2. Surface the tension: why this isn't easy; who disagrees and why.
3. Reframe with the author's specific lens.
4. Show the implication: what the reader does differently now.

## Problem → mistake → diagnosis → method
1. The problem most people in the audience have.
2. The well-meaning mistake that perpetuates it.
3. The author's diagnosis (the frame).
4. The method the author uses — narratively, not as a tutorial.

## Story → moral → counter-example
1. A specific story (a person, a moment, an outcome).
2. The lesson, stated plainly.
3. The "but also" — the situation where the lesson fails. Avoids preachiness; builds trust.

## Hooks that earn the page turn
- A specific claim that contradicts conventional wisdom in the field
- A confession from the author's own work
- A question the reader has been carrying but never named
- A scene set in concrete time and place ("It was 2019, and I was sitting in…")

## Endings that hold the book together
- Return to the chapter's opening image with new meaning
- Pose the next question the book will tackle
- Hand the reader something concrete to do or notice
