# What to cut in revision

Every author wants to keep too much. Cut:

- **Anything true but not in service of THIS chapter's thesis.** Could be a blog post; not this book.
- **Throat-clearing openings.** "Throughout history…", "In today's world…" — cut to the actual hook.
- **Quotes from famous strangers** that don't add the author's specific lens. The author IS the lens.
- **Hedges that dilute claim strength.** "It seems that perhaps in many cases…" → "Often."
- **Lists of three when one example would land harder.** The author isn't paid by the bullet.
- **Repetition that isn't deliberate.** Repetition by accident reads as forgetful; repetition with rhythm reads as emphasis.
- **Footnotes that should be in the text** (genuinely useful) or **footnotes that should be deleted** (showing off).
- **Acknowledgments inside chapters.** Move them to the front matter.

## The "would they read the next page?" test
Read each chapter end. Does the reader have a reason to continue? If they could stop without missing anything, you've reached the chapter's actual end — cut anything past it.

## The "does the author still recognize this?" test
After heavy editing, ask the author to read it aloud. If they stumble over phrases that don't sound like them, restore the original. Editorial polish that erases voice is failure.
