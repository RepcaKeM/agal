# Trend Researcher

## Overview

Most "trend research" is googling for confirmation of a bet already made. This skill forces sources, dating, and a falsifiable claim — so the analysis can age into "we were right" or "we were wrong" instead of vibes.

## When to Use

- Scoping a new strategic bet (new market, new product line, new tech adoption)
- Refreshing a competitive landscape before a roadmap decision
- Evaluating an emerging technology (do we adopt now, watch, ignore?)
- Building a one-pager that frames an opportunity for execs

## Iron Law

```
EVERY CLAIM CITES A SOURCE WITH A DATE. NO SOURCE → NO CLAIM.

"Industry is moving to X" without a citation is your hunch.
A blog post from 2019 isn't a 2026 trend. Date-stamp everything.

FRAME OPPORTUNITIES AS HYPOTHESES, NOT CONCLUSIONS.
"We believe X will be true by date Y; we'll know if Z." Then track.
```

## Checklist (trend / market analysis)

1. **The question this report answers** — "should we <do X>?" — written before any research. → check: a decision rides on the answer.
2. **Source quality stack** — primary (interviews, first-party data) > industry analysts > reputable press > vendor marketing > blogs. → check: at least 3 primary or analyst-grade sources per major claim.
3. **Time-bound claims** — every assertion has a date. → check: no undated trend lines.
4. **Triangulation** — at least two independent sources for any load-bearing number. → check: 1-source claims flagged as such.
5. **Counter-narrative** — name the strongest case AGAINST the conclusion. → check: written; if you can't find it, your search has a confirmation bias.
6. **Hypothesis with a test** — what would falsify this in 6 months? → check: 1–3 leading indicators with thresholds.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Vendor whitepapers as evidence.** They sell a worldview. Triangulate.
- **TAM-SAM-SOM with no segmentation.** A $200B market means nothing without "and we can serve which 0.5% of it."
- **Trends-as-product-features list.** "AI everywhere" is a category, not a strategy.
- **Citing analyst-firm reports without reading them.** They often hedge more than the marketing summary suggests.
- **No counter-narrative.** A piece without disagreement is propaganda.
- **Mistaking activity for adoption.** Conference mentions ≠ deployments. Tweets ≠ revenue.
