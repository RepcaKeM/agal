# Sales Engineer

## Overview

Demos that show every feature sell to no one. POCs without success criteria become science projects. This skill makes you discover the buyer's real technical risk first, and only then design the demo or POC that retires it.

## When to Use

- Preparing for a discovery call or technical deep-dive with a prospect
- Designing a demo for a specific opportunity (not a generic webinar)
- Scoping a POC / pilot — success criteria, timeline, scope
- Building / updating a competitive battlecard
- Responding to a technical objection mid-cycle (security, scale, integration, etc.)

## Iron Law

```
NO DEMO BEFORE DISCOVERY. NO POC WITHOUT SUCCESS CRITERIA.

A demo without discovery is a feature tour. A POC without written
success criteria becomes a hostage situation: the prospect won't sign
because "we still want to test X" forever.

CONFIRM THE ECONOMIC BUYER + TECHNICAL CHAMPION BEFORE INVESTING
POC HOURS. Without both, the POC succeeds technically and dies
in procurement.
```

## Checklist (discovery)

1. **Current state** — what they use today, what breaks. → check: specific tools / workflows / pain points named.
2. **Trigger event** — why they're looking NOW vs last quarter. → check: one sentence; if missing, deal is weakly qualified.
3. **Decision criteria** — what they're using to evaluate, in their own words. → check: written, ranked.
4. **Stakeholders mapped** — economic buyer (signs check), technical buyer (must say yes), champion (sells internally), end user. → check: name per role.
5. **Timeline + budget reality** — when they expect to decide, what they've budgeted. → check: not "ASAP / TBD."

## Checklist (demo design)

1. **One opening claim** matching their #1 pain. Skip company-history slides.
2. **Demo flow = their workflow.** No screen they wouldn't recognize from their day. → check: storyboarded against their use case.
3. **Wow moments scripted** (2–3) and timed early, not buried at the end.
4. **Handle the elephant** — if you know a weakness will come up, address it before they raise it.
5. **End with the next step**, not "any questions?" → check: written next step on screen ("propose POC scope by Tuesday").

## Checklist (POC scope)

1. **Success criteria signed by the technical champion** — measurable, time-bound. → check: written, dated.
2. **Scope bounded** — what's tested, what's NOT. Anything else = follow-up. → check: explicit "out of scope" list.
3. **Timeline ≤4 weeks** for typical SaaS. If longer, you're rebuilding their stack. → check: weekly checkpoints scheduled.
4. **Mutual commitments** — what their team must do (data, access, attendance), not just yours. → check: bi-directional commitment doc.
5. **Disposition decision rule** — "if criteria pass → sign by date X." → check: written before POC starts.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Demo as feature parade.** Loses the buyer in minute 2.
- **POC scope creep.** Each "while we're at it…" pushes the close by weeks.
- **"Let me build a custom demo environment overnight."** Sets expectation that you'll always do that. Use templates.
- **Battlecard with no evidence.** Naming a competitor's flaw without a source = your opinion. Cite review sites, customer interviews, public docs.
- **Discovery as interrogation.** 30 minutes of questions and no value back; champion ghosts you.
- **Champion-only relationship.** When champion leaves, deal dies. Map and engage at least 2 contacts.
