# Sales Outreach

## Overview

Most cold outreach is template-fill spam with a customized first name. It gets ignored. The rare email that gets a reply names a specific business reason the prospect should care, today, in 60 words or fewer.

## When to Use

- Writing a cold first-touch email to a named account
- Designing a multi-touch sequence (email + LinkedIn + call)
- Crafting an objection response (price / timing / status-quo / "send info")
- Following up on a stalled / ghosted deal
- Drafting a written proposal (after discovery, before contract)

## Iron Law

```
ONE EMAIL = ONE BUSINESS REASON THIS PROSPECT SHOULD CARE TODAY +
ONE LOW-FRICTION ASK. ≤120 words. NO TEMPLATE-SHAPED OPENING.

"I hope this finds you well" / "I'm reaching out because" / "We help
[company-type] [verb] [outcome]" — these flag the email as bulk.
Delete them.

NEVER ASK FOR 30 MINUTES IN THE FIRST EMAIL. The ask must match
the relationship: first email = a yes/no question or a 5-second
look at one thing.
```

## Checklist (cold first-touch)

1. **Trigger** — why now? Funding round, exec hire, product launch, recent press, a specific change at the company. → check: cited; if generic, you're spamming.
2. **Business reason** — what specific cost / opportunity this trigger creates for them. → check: in their language, not yours.
3. **Relevance proof** — one sentence on why YOU are credible to message them about this (similar customer outcome, niche expertise, a fact from their context). → check: not "we help X do Y" generic.
4. **Low-friction ask** — a yes/no question, or "worth a 10-min look?" Not "do you have 30 minutes Tuesday at 2:30 or Wednesday at 11?" → check: prospect can reply in one sentence.
5. **≤120 words. Mobile-readable. No images. No P.S. line longer than 1 sentence.** → check: paste in a phone preview.

## Checklist (multi-touch sequence)

1. **Vary the angle, not just the wording.** Touch 1 = trigger-based. Touch 2 = different angle on same pain. Touch 3 = "social proof." Touch 4 = breakup email. → check: a teammate can describe each touch's purpose.
2. **5–7 touches, spread over 14–21 days.** More than that without a reply = re-qualify the account, don't keep mailing. → check: cadence written.
3. **Channels mixed**: email + LinkedIn comment / DM + phone. Each channel reinforces, doesn't duplicate. → check: not the same message on 3 channels.
4. **Breakup email** is the most effective touch. "Last note — should I close the loop here, or is there a better time to reach back out?" Polite, real out. → check: it's actually a real out, not a guilt trip.

## Checklist (proposal)

1. **Recap their problem** in their words first. → check: they'd recognize the description.
2. **Solution scope** — what you'll do, in plain language. NO product feature dump. → check: a non-technical exec can read it.
3. **Outcome metric** + how it'll be measured. → check: one primary metric, not five.
4. **Investment** — clear price, what's included, what's not. → check: no "starting at" weasel words.
5. **Mutual commitments** — what you do, what they do. → check: bi-directional.
6. **Next step** — concrete action and date. → check: not "let me know."

## Objection responses (one line each)

- **"Send me info."** → "Happy to. Quickly — what's the one thing I should make sure to address? Don't want to spam you with a 30-page deck."
- **"Not a priority right now."** → "Totally fair. What would have to be true for it to become one in the next quarter?"
- **"We use <competitor>."** → "Many of our customers came from there. Mind if I ask what's working well and what isn't?"
- **"Too expensive."** → "What price point would make this an easy yes? Then we can talk about whether the scope fits there."
- **"Send me a proposal."** → "Of course. To make it useful, can I ask 3 quick questions first?"

## Anti-Patterns

- **Personalization tokens that read as automation.** `Hi {{first_name}}, congrats on {{recent_post}}` is worse than no personalization.
- **AI-generated openers about how impressed you are with their LinkedIn.** Universally cringe.
- **The 30-second video.** Few watch it; it screams "scaled outreach."
- **"Just checking in."** Adds nothing. Add a new reason or stop following up.
- **Burning the breakup email and then sending more.** Either commit to the close or don't claim it.
- **Selling to the wrong persona because their title is closest to your buyer.** Wrong buyer never closes; they only delay.
