# Cold email templates — annotated

## Template 1: Trigger-based

```
Subject: <specific trigger> — 30 sec read

Hey <name>,

Noticed <specific trigger — funding, hire, launch, press, public roadmap>.
That usually means <specific consequence for their team>.

We helped <similar company> handle exactly this — they saw <specific outcome>
in <timeframe>. Happy to share the playbook (one page, no slides).

Worth a look?

<you>
```

**Why this works**: trigger is specific, consequence is plausible, proof is named, ask is low-friction.

## Template 2: Peer-mention

```
Subject: <peer company name> — quick question

<name>,

<peer company> just <specific action> using <relevant capability>.
Looking at <your company>'s <relevant context>, I'm curious whether
you're thinking about something similar — or if you've already
solved this another way.

If it's interesting, I can send a 2-paragraph teardown of what
<peer> did. If not, no worries.

<you>
```

**Why**: peer mention signals relevance; teardown ask is asymmetric (you do the work, they just say yes/no).

## Template 3: Pattern-interrupt

```
Subject: Wrong person?

<name>,

I might be wrong, but is <topic> still part of your remit at <company>?
If yes — there's a thing I'd love your 60-second take on.
If no — could you point me to whoever owns it?

Thanks either way.
<you>
```

**Why**: low cognitive load; permission-based; honest. Surprisingly effective.

## What NOT to send

- "I hope this email finds you well." (Filtered to spam by the brain.)
- "I'm reaching out to learn more about your team." (Vague, expects them to do the work.)
- "We help [vertical] companies [verb] [generic outcome]." (Reads as fill-in-the-blank.)
- 5-paragraph value-prop dump. (Mobile-unreadable.)
