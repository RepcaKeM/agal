# Cover letter: structure and example

Four short paragraphs, 150 words at most.

| # | Paragraph | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solution | What you would build or do for this job: approach, stack, outcome. 1–2 sentences. No restating their problem. |
| 2 | Proof | One matching case: what was built, for whom (industry + country), one number if the evidence has one. Portfolio line last, verbatim, on its own line, if the caller gave one (not counted). |
| 3 | Plan | 2–3 numbered steps specific to this job. |
| 4 | Question | One question the client must answer to move forward. |

## Openings to avoid

- "Hi, I'm … and I have X years of experience…"
- "I read your job post carefully…"
- "I'd love to help you with…"
- "Dear Hiring Manager"
- Any sentence about yourself before the solution.
- Restating the job post ("You need…", "Your team spends…") — the client wrote it.

## Example (101 words + portfolio line)

Job: n8n workflow that enriches inbound leads with GPT and pushes them to HubSpot.

> I'd build this as one n8n workflow: GPT researches every new lead, fills in the
> company details and writes them to HubSpot before your sales team opens the record.
>
> I run the same pattern in production for a B2B print company: n8n pulls new
> contacts nightly, an LLM fills in company details, and results land in their CRM
> for review.
> Portfolio: example.com - more proofs and examples of my work.
>
> Plan: 1) map your form fields to HubSpot properties, 2) build and test the
> enrichment on 20 real leads, 3) switch it on with error alerts to Slack.
>
> Which fields does your team actually use when deciding who to call first?
